DRONE MATRXDESIGN LANGUAGE SPECIFICATIONVersion 1.0Internal Engineering ReferenceApplies to: Web application, dashboard modules, and future feature pagesAudience: Product designers, frontend engineers, AI coding assistants1. Introduction &amp; Design Philosophy1.1 PurposeThis document defines the official visual and interaction design language of the Drone Matrx platform. It is derived directly from the existing product interface and serves as the single source of truth for all future page development. Every rule in this document is mandatory. Deviations are not permitted without a formal update to this specification.1.2 Product IdentityDrone Matrx is a professional drone fleet management and mission control platform. The interface is built for operators who plan flight missions, monitor live telemetry, manage fleet inventory, and review mission analytics. The product identity is tactical, technical, and mission-critical rather than consumer-facing or decorative.1.3 Target UsersFleet operators who launch, monitor, and terminate drone missions.Mission planners who configure flight paths, payloads, and waypoints.Analysts who review flight reports, telemetry logs, and fleet status.System administrators who manage fleet inventory and platform settings.1.4 Interface PersonalityThe interface communicates precision, control, and situational awareness. It borrows visual conventions from military and aerospace command consoles: dark surfaces, monospaced-feeling numeric displays, angular typography, compass and radar motifs, and high-contrast status indicators. The tone is technical and instrumented, never playful or ornamental.1.5 Usability GoalsCritical status information (mission state, alerts, time) is visible at all times in the top navigation bar.Live operational views (maps, telemetry) occupy the majority of the viewport with controls arranged at the periphery.Every interactive control has a single, unambiguous purpose and a fixed screen position.Dense information panels remain legible through consistent contrast and spacing rather than reduced information density.1.6 Visual DirectionThe visual direction is dark-surface, high-contrast, and instrumentation-driven. Backgrounds use desaturated navy, charcoal, and slate tones. Typography uses a condensed, angular display face for headings and labels to reinforce a technical, stenciled, military-console aesthetic. Color is reserved for status communication (active, alert, informational) rather than decoration.2. Brand Identity &amp; Color System2.1 Color PhilosophyDrone Matrx uses a restrained, low-saturation palette dominated by navy, charcoal, and slate greys. Color is functional: it distinguishes surface levels (primary navigation vs. content panels vs. cards) and communicates status (active, pending, alert). Bright or saturated colors are avoided outside of status indicators. Navigation &amp; Page Architecture4.1 Top Navigation BarThe top navigation bar is present on every page without exception and is fixed to the top of the viewport. It contains, from left to right: a menu (hamburger) icon, the Drone Matrx logo mark, the wordmark &quot;DRONE MATRX&quot;, followed by a right-aligned cluster containing a search control, contextual page-level menu items (e.g. MISSION, STATUS), an ALERT indicator, and a live digital clock.Background: --dm-charcoal-950 on the landing page; --dm-navy-900 on all interior/operational pages.Text and icon color: white, at full opacity.Height: fixed, does not resize based on page content.The clock and ALERT indicator are always present and always right-aligned, in that order, reading left to right as: SEARCH → contextual page actions → ALERT → clock.4.2 Sidebar NavigationThe primary navigation sidebar is triggered by the hamburger icon in the top navigation bar and overlays the left portion of the current page as a slide-in panel; it does not push page content. The sidebar background is a semi-transparent dark overlay over the existing page background.Items: MISSION, FLIGHT PLAN, ANALYSE, FLEET, REPORTS, SETTINGS, in this fixed order.Each item is preceded by a right-facing chevron/arrow glyph.Sidebar item typography matches the condensed stencil display face used in the top navigation wordmark.Only one navigation level is present; there are no nested/expandable sidebar sub-items.4.3 Active &amp; Hover StatesActive navigation items are distinguished by full-opacity white text against the overlay background; inactive items may render at reduced opacity.Hover states darken or lighten the background band directly behind the item without changing its horizontal position or introducing an outline.4.4 Page HeadersEach operational page begins with a full-width title bar in --dm-slate-700 containing the page name in uppercase followed by an ellipsis (e.g. &quot;MISSIONS...&quot;). This title bar sits directly beneath the top navigation bar and above all content panels.4.5 Content ContainersPrimary content is organized into rectangular panels with --dm-navy-900 or --dm-slate-700 backgrounds, rounded corners per --dm-radius-md, and consistent internal padding.Map and telemetry views use a three-column layout: a left control rail, a center full-bleed map viewport, and a right instrument rail (compass, 3D toggle, zoom controls).List/table views use a two-column layout: a left column of stacked summary/action cards and a right column containing the primary data table.4.6 Layout Rules by Device ClassDeviceBehaviorDesktop (≥1200px)Full three-region layout; sidebar overlays as a fixed-width panel; instrument rail visible on map views.Tablet (768–1199px)Two-column layouts collapse to a single column with cards stacked above the data table; instrument rail remains docked right but narrows.Mobile (&lt;768px)Single-column stacking throughout; top navigation bar retains menu, logo, and clock only, with search and secondary actions moved behind the menu overlay.5. Components Library5.1 ButtonsPurpose: trigger primary and secondary actions (START, REPLAY, search, view controls). Appearance: solid fill using a surface color one step lighter or darker than its parent container, uppercase label, condensed display typeface, --dm-radius-sm or --dm-radius-pill corners. States: default, hover (background shifts one step in luminance), disabled (reduced opacity, no hover response).Usage rule: primary actions (START) always appear to the left of secondary actions (REPLAY) within the same control group.Usage rule: icon-only buttons (zoom in/out, directional pad, 3D toggle) are always circular or square with a centered glyph and no visible label.5.2 CardsPurpose: group related summary information (mission thumbnail, status, drone report). Appearance: rectangular container, --dm-radius-md corners, solid surface background, internal padding of --dm-space-md. States: static; cards in this product do not currently expose hover elevation changes. Usage rule: each card addresses exactly one subject (one mission, one drone, one status) and never mixes unrelated data types.5.3 Inputs &amp; Search BarsPurpose: text entry and filtering. Appearance: pill-shaped or slightly rounded rectangular field with placeholder text in reduced-opacity white or grey, no visible border, background one step lighter than the surrounding panel. States: default (placeholder visible), focused (cursor active, placeholder remains until typed), filled. Usage rule: the global search control in the top navigation bar is always represented as a circular icon button paired with the label &quot;+ SEARCH&quot;.5.4 Dropdowns / FiltersPurpose: narrow table results (FILTER, STATUS, OWNER, DATE). Appearance: identical pill styling to text inputs, with a trailing ellipsis in the placeholder to indicate expandable choice. Usage rule: filter controls are always arranged in a single horizontal row directly above the data table they affect, in a fixed left-to-right order: FILTER → STATUS → OWNER → DATE.5.5 TablesPurpose: present structured records (missions, fleet inventory, reports). Appearance: header row in --dm-slate-700 with bold uppercase white labels; body rows alternate between --dm-slate-600 and a slightly lighter tint; no vertical gridlines; thin horizontal dividers between rows. Usage rule: the first column always contains a visual/thumbnail reference where one is available (e.g. a map preview); the final column always contains an owner/responsible-party indicator.5.6 Tags, Badges &amp; Status IndicatorsPurpose: communicate discrete state at a glance. Appearance: pill-shaped, --dm-radius-pill, centered uppercase text, background drawn from the semantic status palette (2.3). States: ACTIVE (olive-tinted background), PENDING/ETA (neutral grey background). Usage rule: a status pill never contains more than one word or short phrase; multi-part status detail (e.g. &quot;ETA 12:45&quot;) is split into a label and a value beneath it, not crowded into one pill.5.7 Alerts &amp; NotificationsPurpose: surface time-critical system messages. Appearance: represented in the top navigation bar as a persistent &quot;ALERT&quot; label; page-level alerts, when present, use the same navy/slate surface language with white text and are positioned at the top of the content region, above all panels. Usage rule: the ALERT control in the top bar is always visible and never hidden behind a menu, regardless of device class.5.8 Dialogs, Tooltips &amp; Progress BarsPurpose: modal confirmation, contextual hints, and progress/telemetry visualization (e.g. the horizontal altitude/distance bar in the map footer). Appearance: dialogs use the --dm-navy-900 surface with white text and centered placement; the telemetry progress bar is a thin horizontal track in a lighter tint against a darker footer bar, with the numeric value set in the monospaced telemetry face immediately adjacent. Usage rule: any live-updating numeric readout is paired with its unit of measure directly beside the value (e.g. &quot;50M&quot;, &quot;92M&quot;, &quot;97.3M&quot;).6. Drone Matrx Specific UI Patterns6.1 Mission CardsA Mission Card combines a map thumbnail, a status pill, and either a countdown/ETA value or an action control (START / REPLAY). Mission Cards are stacked vertically in the left column of the Missions page and always appear in this fixed internal order: map thumbnail, status label, timing/action row.6.2 Drone Report CardA compact card presenting a single drone's identity: a line-art drone icon, a NAME field, and a MODEL NO field. This card uses the darkest surface tone (--dm-navy-900) to visually anchor it as a fixed reference card distinct from the scrollable mission list above it.6.3 Fleet &amp; Mission Data TableThe primary data table pattern used across Missions, Fleet, and Reports pages. Columns are fixed per page type but always begin with a visual reference column and always end with an owner/responsible-party column, per rule 5.5.6.4 Live Telemetry PanelThe left control rail on the Flight Plan / map view. It stacks discrete setting cards (payload, flight distance, flight duration, takeoff point, altitude, fine-adjustment sliders) in a single scrollable column. Each card has an uppercase title ending in an ellipsis, followed by one or two data rows presenting the current value.6.5 Live Map ViewportThe center region of map-based views. It renders a full-bleed satellite map with overlaid drone position markers (line-art top-down drone icons), points-of-interest pins, and a single primary target/launch marker (green pin). The map viewport has no visible border or card framing; it extends to the edges of the content region.6.6 Instrument Rail (Compass / 3D / Zoom)The right rail on map views. It stacks, top to bottom: a compass rose widget, a 3D toggle control with an associated slider, a four-directional pan pad, and zoom in/out controls. This rail is always docked to the right edge of the viewport and never relocates to the left.6.7 Telemetry Footer BarA full-width bar fixed to the bottom of map views, presenting a horizontal progress/altitude track alongside labeled numeric readouts (e.g. IND, HAE) and a reference standard label (e.g. WGS 84). This bar uses --dm-slate-400/--dm-slate-500 tones distinct from the darker navigation and control surfaces.6.8 Live Tiles / Operation BannerA small persistent banner within the control rail identifying the current live operation by name (e.g. an operation codename) beneath a &quot;LIVE TILES&quot; label. This banner is always the final element at the bottom of the left control rail.7. Responsive Design Guidelines7.1 BreakpointsBreakpoint TokenRangeTarget--dm-bp-desktop≥ 1200pxFull desktop layout--dm-bp-laptop992px – 1199pxCompact desktop / laptop layout--dm-bp-tablet768px – 991pxTablet layout--dm-bp-mobile&lt; 768pxMobile layout7.2 Desktop LayoutAll three layout regions (top navigation, primary content, instrument rail) render simultaneously. Sidebar overlays on demand without shifting content. Data tables display all columns without truncation.7.3 Laptop LayoutIdentical region structure to desktop with reduced horizontal padding and a narrower instrument rail. No columns are dropped from data tables at this range.7.4 Tablet LayoutThe left control rail and right instrument rail collapse into collapsible panels accessible via toggle controls rather than persistent columns. Data tables retain all columns but reduce cell padding.7.5 Mobile LayoutSingle-column stacking applies throughout. The top navigation bar is reduced to menu icon, logo, and clock; search and contextual actions move into the sidebar overlay. Data tables convert each row into a stacked card presenting label/value pairs rather than a horizontal row, preserving the fixed first-column (visual) and last-column (owner) rule from 5.5 as the top and bottom elements of the stacked card respectively.8. Developer Implementation Guidelines8.1 CSS Variable NamingAll design tokens are namespaced with the prefix dm- and grouped by category: color (--dm-&lt;surface&gt;-&lt;step&gt;), spacing (--dm-space-&lt;size&gt;), radius (--dm-radius-&lt;size&gt;), and breakpoint (--dm-bp-&lt;device&gt;). Category order in the token name must not be altered.8.2 Component NamingComponents are named after the product concept they represent, not their visual shape, using PascalCase: MissionCard, DroneReportCard, FleetDataTable, TelemetryPanel, InstrumentRail, StatusPill, FilterBar. Generic names such as Box, Panel1, or Card2 are not permitted.8.3 HTML/Markup StructureEvery page renders exactly one top navigation landmark, one page-title bar, and one primary content region.Sidebar navigation is implemented as an overlay element outside the normal document flow of the content region, not as a flex/grid sibling that resizes content.Data tables use semantic table markup (table/thead/tbody/tr/th/td); card-based mobile fallbacks are a CSS presentation change, not a markup change.8.4 Accessibility RulesMinimum text contrast ratio of 4.5:1 must be maintained between text and its background at all times; white-on-navy and black-on-hero-sage combinations already satisfy this and must not be altered.All icon-only buttons (search, zoom, pan, 3D toggle) require an accessible label even though no visible text label is present.Status pills communicate state through both color and text label; color alone is never the sole indicator of status.Live-updating regions (clock, telemetry values) are marked as polite live regions so assistive technology is not interrupted on every update.8.5 Consistency RulesA component defined in this specification must be reused wherever its underlying concept recurs; new one-off components must not be created for concepts already covered in Section 5 or Section 6.Any new page must reuse the existing top navigation bar, page-title bar pattern, and spacing scale without modification.8.6 Reusable Component PhilosophyEvery visual pattern in the product is treated as a shared component with a defined props/state contract rather than a page-specific block of markup. New pages are assembled from existing components in Section 5 and Section 6; a new component is introduced only when no existing component can represent the required concept.9.
